What QCC does to an allelic-imbalance point estimate

The AI point estimate never moves. Its confidence interval — and whether the gene gets called at all — is set by the overdispersion coefficient QCC (per sample: iQCC). After Mendelevich 2021 Fig. 1a/3 and Mendelevich 2023 Fig. 1a–e. AI = maternal / (maternal + paternal).

What the page computes

Observed allelic counts are modelled as an extended beta-binomial with Q = QCC² (Mendelevich 2023, §2.1.1): m ~ eBB(n, AI, Q) with shape δ = (Q−1)/(n−Q), α = AI/δ, β = (1−AI)/δ.

Var(m) = QCC² · n · AI(1−AI)   ⇒   SD(AI) = QCC · √(AI(1−AI)/n)

So QCC is exactly a widening coefficient on the binomial quantiles, and it is equivalent to having sequenced n / QCC² allelic reads. Tests and intervals follow Mendelevich 2021 Eq. 11: run R's prop.test (Wilson score interval with continuity correction) on counts divided by QCC². Both are reimplemented here in JavaScript and checked against R (Yates correction, CI construction, and p-values agree to six significant figures). In scenario 2 each sample is deflated by its own iQCC², as controlFreq does — so a single noisy library limits the comparison on its own.

  • The point estimate never changes — AI = m/(m+p), independent of QCC. Only the interval around it does.
  • Scenario 1 tests H₀: AI = 0.5 (one-sample). Scenario 2 tests H₀: AI₀₁ = AI₂ (two-sample), the differential-AI test.
  • Non-overlapping CIs is a stricter criterion than the two-sample test at the same confidence level; the page reports the test, and the plot shows the CIs.
  • Reported QCC/iQCC in these papers ranges roughly 1.4–2.6 across replicate pairs, with iQCC > 4 seen in the noisiest libraries — a 16-fold overstatement of effective coverage by the naive binomial test.
